2. Initial decision-tree drafts
These are based on your documented cases. They are field-derived drafts, not substitutes for manufacturer troubleshooting procedures.
Tree A — Tekmar outdoor temperature incorrect or unstable
Applies initially to the 256 and 274; adapt after checking each model's manual.
START: Displayed outdoor temperature is incorrect


1. Identify the sensor source
- Is the controller configured for a physical outdoor sensor or an external/network weather source?
- Compare the displayed reading with an independent outdoor temperature measurement.
- Record the exact controller model and settings.


2. Inspect the reading over time
- Stable but incorrect: verify sensor specifications, resistance-versus-temperature values, configuration, and sensor placement.
- Rapid jumps: inspect wiring connections, moisture, shorts, intermittent opens, and electrical interference.
- Missing or invalid reading: verify the selected input or configured weather source.


3. Test against the manufacturer's sensor chart

Measure resistance only with the circuit isolated as directed by the manual. Compare the measurement at the measured sensor temperature with the correct model's resistance chart. Do not assume all Tekmar sensors share the same resistance curve.


4. Verify recovery

Confirm the outdoor reading is stable and plausible, review the reset curve and WWSD settings, and verify boiler operation using the approved test procedure.

Related case: Tekmar 256, October 5, 2026. The outdoor reading changed from approximately 65°F to 82–85°F. The UP-button firing behavior was also reported; verify its intended test-mode behavior in the exact manual.

Tree B — B&G Ecocirc E02 / E06 faults
START: Pump stops or requires manual reset


1. Record the fault before resetting

Record the exact model, full displayed code, operating conditions, supply voltage, and whether the fault occurs during startup or normal operation.

E02
High motor current
- Isolate power before inspection.
- Check for mechanical obstruction or binding according to the service manual.
- Inspect motor/pump condition and supply conditions.
- If the fault persists, follow the manufacturer's service and replacement criteria.


E06
Reported voltage imbalance
- Measure the incoming supply using qualified electrical procedures.
- Check all applicable phases, connections, and supply conditions.
- Compare measurements with the exact model's allowable limits.
- Investigate the power source if the fault correlates with generator operation.



3. Confirm the cause before returning to service

Correct the identified issue, clear the fault only as instructed, and observe the pump through startup and normal operation. Do not treat a successful power cycle as proof that the underlying fault is resolved.

E02 and E06 are based on the cases you described. Exact fault descriptions, reset instructions, and electrical limits need citations from the applicable Ecocirc manual.

Tree C — AERCO BME external interlock alarm
START: Boiler reports an external interlock alarm


1. Establish the alarm state

Record the exact alarm text, boiler designation, controller revision, operating state, and any active BMS commands. Determine whether the alarm is active, latched, or historical according to the controller documentation.


2. Verify the interlock circuit

A continuity reading alone may not establish correct operation under operating conditions.
- Follow the unit-specific wiring diagram.
- Verify the prescribed signal and circuit state using approved electrical procedures.
- Inspect field wiring, terminals, external contacts, and BMS logic.
- Compare controller configuration with the documented installation.


3. Investigate configuration only when justified

Back up existing parameters before any authorized reset or restoration. Compare settings with the approved configuration and investigate whether a reset is appropriate under AERCO's procedure.

4. Verify restoration

Confirm the interlock operates correctly, alarms clear through the prescribed procedure, and the boiler operates normally with all safety circuits intact. Never bypass the interlock to force operation.

Related case: BME boiler tagged 2A, October 20, 2026. The controller program was backed up, parameters reset and restored, and the uncleared interlock alarms subsequently cleared. That sequence is an observed outcome, not proof that every similar alarm is a programming fault.
